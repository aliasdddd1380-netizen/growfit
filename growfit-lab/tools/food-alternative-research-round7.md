# Round 7 — additional Iranian flatbread mineral studies and evidence screening

Review date: 2026-11-14

## Executive outcome

This round expanded the bread evidence inventory and separated nutritional composition studies from food-contamination studies.

A 2005 Tehran study measured zinc in 36 traditional flatbread samples and reports mean zinc concentrations for Lavash, Barbari, Taftoon and Sangak. This is a useful historical lead, but it reports mg/kg and does not provide a matched moisture/as-eaten basis in the accessible abstract. It cannot be normalized into the GrowFit per-100g edible-portion bank without the full method and basis.

A 2023 Isfahan study analyzed 93 samples (31 each of Barbari, Lavash and Taftoon) for both essential elements and toxic elements. The bread samples were oven-dried before acid digestion and reported values are dry-weight concentrations. Its objective was contaminant and exposure-risk assessment; the data must not be treated as a direct food-composition profile. It did not sample Sangak.

The 2022 systematic review includes several other local bread studies (zinc, copper, potassium, magnesium, iron), but some primary records are difficult to retrieve and several have unclear sampling/basis information. The review's table is useful for locating primary papers, not as a substitute for them. The earlier correction remains: its potassium row for the 451-sample regional survey is misattributed; the correct primary source is Aalipour 2019.

No values are approved for the food bank.

## 1. Tehran flatbread zinc study (2005)

Jahed Khaniki GR. "Determination of Zinc Contents in Iranian Flat Breads." Pakistan Journal of Nutrition. 2005;4(5):294–297. DOI: 10.3923/pjn.2005.294.297.

Primary article:
https://pjnonline.org/pjn/article/view/261

- 36 flatbread samples collected from Tehran bakeries in 2004.
- Zinc analyzed by flame atomic absorption spectrophotometry.
- Reported mean ± SD:
  - Lavash: 12.17 ± 1.91 mg/kg
  - Barbari: 10.75 ± 2.64 mg/kg
  - Taftoon: 10.41 ± 3.54 mg/kg
  - Sangak: 14.25 ± 3.56 mg/kg
- The accessible abstract does not specify bread-specific sample counts or moisture/as-eaten versus dry-matter basis.

Decision: research-only. Preserve the reported unit and do not convert to mg/100g or publish until the full paper confirms the basis and sampling details.

## 2. Isfahan traditional bread element survey (2023)

Khodaei SM, Esfandiari Z, Sami M, Ahmadi A. "Determination of metal(oids) in different traditional flat breads distributed in Isfahan city, Iran: Health risk assessment study by latin hypercube sampling." Toxicology Reports. 2023;10:382–388. DOI: 10.1016/j.toxrep.2023.02.015.

Full text:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10027551/

- 93 fresh daily-baked bread samples: Barbari n=31, Taftoon n=31, Lavash n=31.
- Samples were oven-dried at 50°C for 48 hours before digestion and analysis.
- ICP-OES and AAS were used to measure essential and toxic elements.
- The study explicitly reports concentrations on a dry-weight basis for exposure calculations.
- It measures contaminants such as Al, As, Cd, Hg and Pb as well as Fe, Mg, Na and Zn.
- Its aim is food contamination and health-risk assessment, not construction of a nutrient composition table.

Decision: do not import contaminant or dry-weight measurements into the food bank. Nutrient elements measured in this study are not a substitute for a validated edible-portion food-composition record.

## 3. Other Iranian bread studies catalogued in the 2022 systematic review

The systematic review reports studies of Zn, Cu, Fe, Mg, Ca and K in Iranian bread. Examples include:
- Shockravi et al.: Sangak zinc and calcium, one bread sample according to review table.
- Badii et al.: Taftoon zinc in control/high-zinc/low-zinc experimental bread formulations.
- Hojati et al.: zinc in Lavash, Barbari, Taftoon and Sangak; primary article metadata and sample/basis details need careful verification.
- Torchi et al.: copper in four traditional breads; sample-size method unclear in review quality assessment.
- Kianpoor et al.: Hamedan bread and wheat metal survey; contamination-focused and includes potentially toxic elements.
- Aalipour 2019: 451 traditional bread samples and potassium/sodium; primary source already documented in Round 3.

The review's own quality assessment notes limitations in some primary studies. Values are not promoted from review tables alone.

Review:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9664613/

## 5. Publication and data-integrity decision

- No bread values from this round are added to `micronutrientsPer100g`.
- Keep raw units, sample counts and sample bases exactly as reported.
- Do not treat toxic-element measurements as nutrient evidence.
- Do not convert dry-weight values to as-eaten values without matching moisture data and documented calculation.
- Do not use review-table values as direct primary-source evidence where the original paper cannot be verified.
- Null remains unknown; it is never changed to zero.
