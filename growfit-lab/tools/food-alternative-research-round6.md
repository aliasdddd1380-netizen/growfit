# Round 6 — Doogh national-table lead and commercial salt studies

Review date: 2026-11-14

## Executive outcome

This round found a useful, previously unrecorded lead for Doogh: a 2023 Iranian paper explicitly states that its food-composition data were extracted from the Iranian Food Composition Table (published by the National Nutrition and Food Technology Research Institute in 2018) and USDA. Its summary table reports Doogh water at 93.8 g/100g and sodium at 421 mg/100g. This is a potentially strong match to the GrowFit Doogh macro profile, but the article does not identify the exact source table row or which database supplied each value. Therefore it remains a secondary lead, not a direct source record and not approved.

A 2025 Iranian laboratory survey of six industrial Doogh products reports salt 1.04 ± 0.04 g/100g and moisture 82.71 ± 4.92 g/100g. This provides a clear as-sampled product-basis salt observation for that small industrial sample set, but it is not a full nutrient profile and does not necessarily represent the GrowFit Doogh formulation.

A 2021 survey of 60 commercial Doogh samples reports a table unit inconsistency: the salt table labels values mg/L while the comparison standard is g/100mL and the abstract says the values are below that standard. Its salt mean must not be used numerically until the unit discrepancy is resolved.

No values are approved for the GrowFit food bank.

## 1. Iranian Food Composition Table lead — foodId 24

Zakerian M, Roudi F, Mahjoub F, Jamialahmadi T, Sahebkar A, Motavasselian M. "The relationship between nutritional facts and temperament of selected Iranians’ frequent food items: a summative content analysis study." 2023. DOI: 10.5114/amsad/171707.

Full text:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10811537/

### What the paper says about its source

- The authors formed a list of 152 foods and retained 110 common foods for analysis.
- They explicitly state that the Iranian Food Composition Table published by the National Nutrition and Food Technology Research Institute in 2018 and USDA were used to extract nutritional facts.
- The paper's summary table lists Doogh at 93.8 g water/100g and sodium 421 mg/100g.
- It does not identify, row by row, whether the Doogh water and sodium values came from the Iranian table or USDA, nor does it reproduce the exact original FCT food code or full record.

### Comparison with GrowFit

The GrowFit Doogh record's macro profile (per 100g) is protein 1.8g, carbohydrate 2.5g and fat 1.0g. The 2023 paper's water value (93.8g/100g) is broadly compatible with a diluted yogurt drink, and its sodium value is a plausible candidate for the original Iranian table record. But source attribution and exact food definition remain unresolved.

Decision: retain as research-only. The next evidence needed is the original 2018 Iranian Food Composition Table row/code for Doogh, including full macro and micronutrient fields, and a confirmation of whether it is plain, salted, carbonated, or a specific commercial formulation.

## 2. Commercial Doogh salt — 2025 laboratory survey

Massomian A, Rashidimehr A, Mohammadi-Nasrabadi F, Khoshtinat K, Esfarjani F. "Salt Contents in Fermented Dairy Products: A Strategic Blueprint for Healthier Intake." Food Science & Nutrition. First published 15 January 2025. DOI: 10.1002/fsn3.4762.

Full text:
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fsn3.4762

- 79 industrial fermented dairy samples from five Tehran food-chain stores were tested in 2024.
- Salt (NaCl) was measured by potentiometric titration after homogenization.
- Moisture was measured by oven drying.
- Doogh: n=6; salt 1.04 ± 0.04 g/100g; moisture 82.71 ± 4.92 g/100g.
- The study reports mean ± standard error from triplicate experiments.

Limitations:
- Only six Doogh samples.
- Industrial brands, not necessarily equivalent to GrowFit's source food.
- Measures salt and moisture, not a complete micronutrient panel.
- Salt is not automatically substituted for sodium in the food bank.

Decision: research-only; useful product variability evidence and a direct as-sampled mass basis, not an approved sodium value.

## 3. Commercial Doogh salt — 2021 study with unit discrepancy

Mohamadi S, et al. "Microbial and Chemical Characteristics of Doogh (Iranian Fermented Milk Drink)." International Journal of Food Science. 2021. DOI: 10.1155/2021/3009795.

Full text:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8709770/

- 60 commercial Doogh samples and 88 Kefir samples were purchased from local vendors in several Iranian cities in Jan–Mar 2021.
- The study tested preservatives, fungi and salt.
- The abstract states that average salt was below the Iranian standard limit of 0.8 g/100.
- Table 2 labels its salt column as mg/L and gives Doogh mean 0.76 ± 0.01, min 0.5, max 0.94. This is dimensionally inconsistent with the standard stated as g/100mL, and the table values cannot safely be interpreted without clarification.
- The paper's methods identify a standard salt test but do not provide a complete nutrient composition panel.

Decision: keep as a bibliographic lead only; do not store its numerical salt result as a normalized nutrient value.
