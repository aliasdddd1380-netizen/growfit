# Round 3 — primary-table verification: Doogh and Iranian breads

Review date: 2026-11-14

## Executive outcome

This round verified the original 2019 regional bread paper and inspected the full 2016 Doogh paper. It also found a citation mismatch in the 2022 systematic review: its Table 4 labels the 451-sample potassium results as “Tabibian et al. [48]”, but its reference [48] is a different 2017 study about vitamin D stability in fortified flatbread. The actual 451-sample study is Aalipour (2019), DOI 10.15171/ijer.2019.11.

No values are approved for the food bank in this round. The bread potassium and sodium values are useful research candidates with sample counts and moisture information; Doogh macros are informative but do not match the existing GrowFit Doogh record. Doogh calcium and phosphorus values are rejected as unreliable due to internal inconsistencies and unclear units.

## 1. Doogh — foodId 24

Primary source: Nateghi L, Yousefi M, Karimi N. *Evaluation and Comparison the Physicochemical Properties Pasteurized Kefir and Doogh*. IJBPAS. 2016;5(1):129–136.

Source: https://www.ijbpas.com/pdf/2016/January/1451214433MS%20IJBPAS%202015%203496.pdf

### Sample design and methods

- The abstract says six samples in total, but the methods clarify that there were three pasteurized Doogh products (A, B, C) and three kefir products (A, B, C), bought from local stores in Tehran.
- pH was measured using a pH meter; acidity by titration; moisture, salt, protein and ash by referenced Iranian standard methods; calcium and phosphorus by a cited method.
- The article does not give a clear nutrient-specific unit/basis statement for all Table 1 values. The table presents percentage-like values, but no explicit as-eaten per-100g declaration.

### Values reported for the three Doogh products

| Measure | A | B | C | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Moisture (%) | 89.71 | 78.66 | 73.78 | Direct table values |
| Protein (%) | 2.33 | 2.71 | 2.80 | Mean about 2.61% |
| Fat (%) | 3.61 | 2.31 | 2.52 | Mean about 2.81% |
| Carbohydrate (%) | 8.03 | 12.76 | 13.97 | Mean about 11.59%; method/basis not fully explained |
| Salt (%) | 1.01 | 0.86 | 1.05 | Mean about 0.97%; do not convert to sodium |
| Ash (%) | 0.66 | 1.02 | 1.34 | Total ash |
| Calcium (%) | 0.91 | 1.13 | 1.09 | Unit/value consistency concern |
| Phosphorus (%) | 0.07 | 1.02 | 1.06 | Unit/value consistency concern |

### Quality assessment and decision

1. The calcium and phosphorus entries are not suitable for publication: the table's reported percentages are extraordinarily high for a diluted dairy drink and, in two samples, their apparent values are difficult to reconcile with the reported total ash. The article's abstract/body summary and table also need careful interpretation. Keep both nutrients null.
2. The paper reports protein around 2.6%, fat around 2.8%, and carbohydrate around 11.6%, which differ substantially from GrowFit's current Doogh base macros (protein 1.8 g, carbohydrate 2.5 g, fat 1 g per 100 g). Using the paper as a direct match would substantially alter energy and macros.
3. Product-to-product variation is large: moisture spans 73.78–89.71%; the three commercial products are not a universal representation of Iranian Doogh.
4. The study's sample count for Doogh is only three products. It is a small comparative study, not a national composition table.

**Decision:** retain the macro values as research-only observations, with raw percentage unit and unresolved basis; do not promote them to the food bank. Calcium and phosphorus are rejected pending a credible independent source. No sodium is inferred from salt.

## 2. Sangak, Barbari and Lavash — foodIds 27, 28, 29

### Primary source verified

Aalipour F. *Evaluation of Salt, Sodium, and Potassium Intake Through Bread Consumption in Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari Province*. International Journal of Epidemiologic Research. 2019;6(2):60–64. DOI: https://doi.org/10.15171/ijer.2019.11

Full text: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334512633_Evaluation_of_Salt_Sodium_and_Potassium_Intake_Through_Bread_Consumption_in_Chaharmahal_and_Bakhtiari_Province

The study collected 451 traditional bread samples in 2016 from bakeries in Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari. Salt was measured potentiometrically; sodium and potassium by flame photometry. Table 1 provides bread-type sample counts, moisture, sodium and potassium.

### Direct Table 1 values

| Bread | n | Moisture (%) | Sodium (mg/100g) | Potassium (mg/100g) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Lavash | 29 | 26.39 ± 3.58 | 608 ± 441 | 103 ± 11 |
| Barbari | 35 | 27.41 ± 1.94 | 550 ± 588 | 112 ± 14 |
| Sangak | 25 | 29.12 ± 1.93 | 663 ± 338 | 110 ± 10 |

The remaining 362 samples were Taftoon (297) and local bread (65). These are not part of the three GrowFit bread IDs.

### Basis and limitations

- The article explicitly reports sodium and potassium in mg per 100 g bread and reports moisture for each bread type.
- It does not explicitly state in the table heading whether the mineral values are on an as-sampled or dry-matter basis. The separate moisture column is not, by itself, permission to convert the mineral values.
- The sample counts for the three breads are modest (Lavash n=29, Barbari n=35, Sangak n=25).
- Standard deviations are large for sodium, especially Lavash and Barbari, indicating high sample variation.
- The values represent a regional sample from one Iranian province and a 2016 collection period, not all bakeries or all recipes in Iran.
- Sodium is measured directly; do not derive it from salt.

**Decision:** accept these as high-value, nutrient-specific research observations, but keep them research-only until the food-bank reviewer confirms the measurement basis and whether a regional average is suitable for the intended GrowFit food definitions. Potassium and sodium only; no inference about other micronutrients.

## 3. Correction to the round 2 bread citation

The 2022 systematic review's Table 4 reproduces the potassium values above but labels the 451-sample source as “Tabibian et al. [48]”. Its own reference list shows [48] as a 2017 article about vitamin D stability in fortified flatbread, not the 451-sample salt/sodium/potassium survey. The primary source for the 451-sample survey is Aalipour (2019), DOI 10.15171/ijer.2019.11. Therefore, future GrowFit provenance should cite Aalipour (2019) as the primary source and treat the systematic review as a secondary discovery source only.

Secondary source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9664613/
