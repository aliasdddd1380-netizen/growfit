# Round 2 — additional research on five unresolved foods

Review date: 2026-11-14

## Outcome

This round adds four useful source leads: a national food-composition compendium for cooked lean lamb leg, a 2020 regional survey of Iranian breads, a 2022 systematic review of Iranian bread nutrient studies, and a 2016 Doogh physicochemical paper that needs full-table verification.

**No food-bank values were published.** All five food records remain under review. This is intentional: useful evidence is not automatically an exact food match.

## 1. Cooked lean lamb — foodId 6

A stronger comparator was located in the UK Food Standards Agency / Royal Society of Chemistry compendium *McCance and Widdowson’s The Composition of Foods* (6th summary edition, 2002).

- Entry 442: whole lamb leg, roasted medium, lean.
- Sample count: 10.
- Basis: per 100 g edible portion, cooked.
- Reported: water 60.5 g, protein 29.7 g, fat 9.4 g, energy 203 kcal.
- Minerals include sodium 63 mg, potassium 360 mg, calcium 7 mg, magnesium 26 mg, phosphorus 220 mg, iron 1.8 mg, copper 0.11 mg, zinc 4.6 mg and selenium 4 µg.
- Vitamins include vitamin D 0.7 µg, thiamin 0.12 mg, riboflavin 0.29 mg, niacin 6.2 mg, B6 0.34 mg and B12 2 µg.

**Decision:** strong comparative evidence, not an exact match. GrowFit's current base macros are 250 kcal, 26 g protein and 16 g fat per 100 g. The compendium's lean roast leg is materially leaner and higher in protein. Do not publish it until the GrowFit food is defined by cut, visible/intermuscular fat and cooking method.

Source: [McCance and Widdowson’s The Composition of Foods (PDF)](https://safeat.ir/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/the-composition-of-foods.pdf)

## 2. Doogh — foodId 24

A 2016 paper comparing pasteurized Doogh and kefir was found. It reports six industrial Doogh samples and appears to contain physicochemical values for protein, carbohydrate, calcium and phosphorus. However, the abstract's extracted sequence does not safely map every value to its nutrient label/unit.

**Decision:** record as a lead only. No values are transcribed until the original tables are checked.

The 2025 fermented-dairy study remains useful for characterization only: six Doogh samples, salt 1.04 ± 0.04 g/100 g and moisture 82.71 ± 4.92 g/100 g. This is not a full micronutrient profile, and salt is not silently converted to sodium.

Sources:
- [Evaluation and Comparison the Physicochemical Properties Pasteurized Kefir and Doogh (2016)](https://www.ijbpas.com/pdf/2016/January/1451214433MS%20IJBPAS%202015%203496.pdf)
- [Salt Contents in Fermented Dairy Products (2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11733677/)

## 3. Sangak, Barbari and Lavash — foodIds 27, 28, 29

### Potassium from a regional Iranian survey

The 2020 study sampled 451 traditional breads in Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari, collected by health inspectors in 2016. Potassium was measured by flame photometry. A 2022 systematic review transcribes bread-specific potassium results:

| Bread | Potassium (mg/100 g) |
|---|---:|
| Sangak | 110 ± 10 |
| Barbari | 112 ± 14 |
| Lavash | 103 ± 11 |

These values are promising nutrient-specific candidates. The primary full-text table, exact sample allocation by bread type and stated sample basis should be checked before any promotion. The study's overall averages (not per bread type) were salt 1.95%, sodium 765 mg/100 g and potassium 108 mg/100 g; those overall averages must not be assigned to each bread.

Sources:
- [Primary regional bread survey (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/c4cadd62c4694fa3bea186c60521eb5a)
- [2022 systematic review (full text)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9664613/)

### Iron, zinc, manganese and copper in Sangak and Barbari

The 2012 study reports mineral values for specific flour extraction rates:

| Bread | Flour extraction | Iron (µg/g) | Zinc (µg/g) | Manganese (µg/g) | Copper (µg/g) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sangak | 93% | 37.6 ± 0.7 | 19.4 ± 2.1 | 27.5 ± 4.7 | 3.7 ± 0.07 |
| Barbari | 82% | 17.5 ± 1.3 | 8.8 ± 0.6 | 8.0 ± 2.6 | 2.7 |

**Decision:** research-only. The accessible table does not establish a compatible moisture/edible-portion basis. Do not convert these values to per-100 g or publish them until the full methods establish the basis.

Source: [Dephytinisation of Sangak and Barbari bread (2012)](https://academic.oup.com/ijfst/article/47/11/2252/7865861)

## 4. Cross-source interpretation

A 2022 systematic review screened ten Iranian bread studies and found analyses covering iron, potassium, magnesium, calcium, copper and zinc. It is valuable as a discovery index, but each result must be traced to its primary study and checked for food identity, sample count, preparation and basis.

Source: [Evaluation of nutrients in bread: a systematic review (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9664613/)
